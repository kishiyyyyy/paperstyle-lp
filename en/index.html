Screens can be too bright

Text on paper reaches your eyes as reflected light. Text on a screen is light coming straight at you. You may be reading the same words, but your body receives them differently. When a screen starts to feel glaring towards the end of the day, that difference may be part of why.

Turning the brightness down does reduce glare. But unless the room is dark, a screen turned down too far only becomes hard to read. Changing the colour temperature leaves white as white, and the gap between black and white as wide as it was. Most people stop adjusting somewhere around there.

Dark mode is a way to put less white on screen. It helps in places, but white text and saturated colour on a dark ground can feel stronger rather than weaker. Documents and the web still open on a white page.

Paperscreen touches neither brightness nor colour temperature. It lays one very thin paper surface over the screen. The hardness of white loosens a little, and the edges of the letters stay exactly where they were.

The white you are reading right now is under that sheet. Take it off with the switch above, or with ⌃⇧P, and you can see how bright your ordinary white had been.

what it does, and doesn't

It does not dim your screen.

There are already several ways to cut glare. Paperscreen overlaps with none of them. It has a different job.

Brightness
Your display's backlight handles this. Paperscreen does not touch it.
Colour over the day
Night Shift handles this. Paperscreen never looks at the clock. Run both.
Contrast and texture
This is the only part Paperscreen handles. Blacks lift a little, the distance between light and dark closes, and a very faint grain sits on the surface.

White may lose at most 10% of its brightness — that is a rule the app is built to, and tested against. There is no layer that dims the whole screen. If dimming were the answer, turning the brightness down would already be enough.

why paper

As calm as a printed page.

A paper surface has fine unevenness, and slight variation in its colour, that an emissive screen does not. That may be part of why it stays comfortable to read for hours.

Paperscreen lays those qualities over the screen, very faintly. Legibility stays where it was; only the hardness of white and the glare are eased. When you stop noticing the effect, that is the right strength.

SURFACE

Only three to choose from.

Chosen by which is easiest to look at rather than by taste, so there are three with distinct characters and no more. Hover a button to try it on this whole page; click to keep it. The app's menu works the same way.

CLEAR

Texture only, colour untouched.

The standard surface: fine grain and almost nothing else. It keeps colour shift to a minimum, so it suits daytime work with photographs and design.

WARM

Loosens the hardness of white.

A faintly warm ground beneath the page, easing the hardness of white backgrounds. For the hours you spend reading — the web, Notion, mail, PDFs.

QUIET

Quiets the distance between light and dark.

Built on a pale grey, making contrast and saturation feel gentler. For the evening, or whenever the screen feels like too much.

how to use it

One minute of setup.

1

Choose a surface

Compare Clear, Warm and Quiet on the same screen and take whichever looks most natural. The menu stays open while you choose, so you can keep going back and forth.

2

Set the intensity

Bring the slider down to just before the point where you stop noticing the effect. If you can see a pattern, it is too strong.

3

Leave it on and forget it

Turn on Open at Login if you want it — it starts out off. Press ⌃⇧P at any time to get your ordinary screen back.

specifics

Small, and quiet.

It doesn't read your screen
Paperscreen never looks at what is on the display. It lays one sheet on top and has no idea what is underneath. It does not ask for screen recording permission.
Local only
Everything happens on your Mac. Paperscreen makes no network connections. There is nothing for it to send.
No account
No sign-up and no login. Install it and start.
Invisible to captures
Screenshots, screen recordings, screen sharing on a video call — Paperscreen is excluded from every software capture path. How you have set your own screen to look stays out of other people's meetings and files.
Light to run
CPU use is essentially zero while nothing is moving. There are no animations and no timers; it redraws only when you change the surface. Memory scales with screen area: roughly 50 MB plus four bytes per physical pixel per display (about 103 MB for a Retina and a 4K together).
It stays out of the way
Clicks, scrolling and keystrokes pass straight through to whatever is underneath. It never takes focus.
Multiple displays
It covers every connected display, and comes back after you unplug and replug one.
Reversible at any moment
⌃⇧P turns it off instantly. Quit the app and nothing is left on the screen.
